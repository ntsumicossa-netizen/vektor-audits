Findings
01

A spa on your own street gets the top pick.

Asked for the best med spa in Garden Oaks, Google's AI names Aesthetic Atelier at 1717 W 34th St as the neighborhood's top choice, then Artavi Med Spa and Dream Aesthetics. Asked about peptide therapy and IV drips near Garden Oaks, your lead services, it again names Aesthetic Atelier first, with Rose Milk Aesthetics, NeuMed and The DRIPBaR Heights. RVRS Med isn't named in either answer.

Source · Google AI Mode, October 20, 2026
02

Say the name out loud and you disappear.

People hear "RVRS" as "Reverse." Ask Google's AI for "reverse med spa Houston" and it says no med spa operates under that name, then recommends NakedMD, River Oaks Galleria Medspa, River Oaks MedSpa and Dream Aesthetics. Regular Google search goes further and rewrites "RVRS Med Houston" as "RVRS Medical Houston."

Source · Google AI Mode and Google Search, October 20, 2026
03

For Botox, you're in one answer and out of the next.

Asked where to get Botox in the Heights or Garden Oaks, Google's AI listed RVRS Med sixth of six. Asked the same question again minutes later, it left you out. An answer that flips between runs means the AI has too little to go on.

Source · Google AI Mode, October 20, 2026
04

No clinicians on your website.

Your site names the co-founders and COO, but no medical director, injector or nurse, and no credentials. In medical aesthetics, AI tools weigh who performs the treatment when deciding whom to recommend.

Source · rvrsmed.com
05

The site shows membership prices only.

Your two memberships ($199 and $250 a month) are listed. Individual treatments have no prices, and the site has no reviews or FAQ. Price and first-visit questions are what new patients ask AI most, and it answers them with sources that publish the details.

Source · rvrsmed.com
