Findings
01

Both AIs recommend everyone but you.

Across four booking questions, Google's AI named 12 companies, led by Omega Royal Transportation and Houston Luxury Rides. ChatGPT's first pick was C&S Executive Transportation for chauffeurs, airport runs and Sprinters, citing a 4.9 rating from 261 reviews. It also named Royal Carriages, EPH Limousine and Blacklane. Houston VIP Rides wasn't named in any of the eight answers.

Source · Google AI Mode and ChatGPT, October 20, 2026
02

There's no phone number.

Airport pickups and wedding bookings are phone-first purchases. Your site offers a form, a Gmail address and Instagram. To a customer and to AI, a missing phone and a free email address read as unestablished.

Source · viprideshouston.com
03

Your site contradicts itself on fleet size.

The stats bar says 2 vehicles in the fleet. The page then shows three: the Sprinter, the Escalade and the S-Class. The booking steps also only mention two. AI will report whichever number it reads first.

Source · viprideshouston.com
04

Airports and areas aren't named.

The FAQ says "all major Houston airports." People don't ask that way. They ask for a car from IAH or Hobby, to the Galleria, to the Medical Center. Without those names in text, you can't match those questions.

Source · viprideshouston.com FAQ
05

Your price lives on Instagram, not your site.

Asked about Houston VIP Rides by name, Google's AI quoted "$200 one-way Sprinter service" from your Instagram, because your website only offers quotes. It also suggested people might mean Houston VIP Taxi, a metered cab company.

Source · Google AI Mode; viprideshouston.com; @viprideshouston
